## Summary

- **Context (not from the Notebook)** Cash application is the finance back-office job of matching money that arrives in the bank to the customer invoices it pays off. It matters because the result decides which invoices look paid, which customers appear to owe money, and what the collections team chases next.
- **Fact** This notebook is version 3 of that process: it reads the day's bank receipts, works out which customer sent each payment, and matches the money against that customer's unpaid invoices. ## How it works in business terms

- **Fact** It loads five input files from the source folder: the bank receipts, the list of unpaid invoices, the customer master, a manual overrides file, and foreign-exchange rates. [Cell 6, line 48](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=6&start=48&end=48) [Cell 8, line 67](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=8&start=67&end=67) [Cell 9, line 80](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=9&start=80&end=80) [Cell 13, line 131](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=13&start=131&end=131) [Cell 16, line 169](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=16&start=169&end=169)
- **Fact** Receipts are cleaned up (thousands separators stripped from amounts, dates accepted in two formats, currency codes trimmed), and only positive receipts dated on or before the run date are kept. Each receipt is tied to a company through its bank account, and receipts on accounts that are not in the mapping are dropped. [Cell 6, lines 49-51](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=6&start=49&end=51) [Cell 7, line 57](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=7&start=57&end=57) [Cell 7, lines 58-60](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=7&start=58&end=60) [Cell 4, line 36](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=4&start=36&end=36)
- **Fact** Customers are grouped into 'pools' (a family of companies that can pay each other's invoices), using the parent company number when one exists. [Cell 9, line 81](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=9&start=81&end=81)
- **Fact** Who paid each receipt is decided in a strict priority order: a manual override file wins, then invoice numbers found in the payment's remittance text (eight-digit numbers starting with a 9), then a cleaned-up comparison of the payer's name against customer names and aliases. The winning source is recorded as OV, REF or NM. ## Potential issues

- **Concern (medium)** The matching step labelled 'FIFO' does not pay the oldest invoices first: it actually goes largest outstanding balance first. The old, now disabled, version-2 logic did order by due date, so anyone reading lines labelled FIFO will expect oldest-first and could draw the wrong conclusion about which invoices were cleared. [Cell 26, line 320](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=26&start=320&end=320) [Cell 26, line 327](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=26&start=327&end=327) [Cell 15, line 158](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=15&start=158&end=158)
- **Concern (medium)** A $250 cap on write-offs is defined but never used anywhere: nothing limits how much the run writes off in total, even though the constant suggests a cap was intended. [Cell 4, line 34](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=4&start=34&end=34) [Cell 26, lines 330-335](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=26&start=330&end=335)
- **Concern (medium)** When a payment's references or payer name point to more than one customer, the notebook simply keeps one of them, the first it happens to find, and the ambiguity is only counted, never stopped or escalated. Cash can land on the wrong customer. [Cell 13, line 126](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=13&start=126&end=126) [Cell 13, line 129](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=13&start=129&end=129) [Cell 19, line 202](/source?path=examples%2Fcash_application_engine.py&cell=19&start=202&end=202)
- **Concern (medium)** The per-receipt reconciliation is largely self-fulfilling: the step just before it forces each receipt's lines back to the banked amount by plugging any difference into the last line. The check therefore mostly re-verifies the plug, and a genuine break would be quietly absorbed into a line rather than surfaced. Whether the GL mapping covers every line type, who receives the notification, and what the log records cannot be verified from this file.
